# Vertical Clay Reclaim Stack: Two Buckets, a Pillowcase and a Terracotta Pot

A compact reclaim and water-recovery system for the studio, stacked vertically so it takes one bucket's footprint. Dirty throwing water goes in the top, clay collects as a filter cake in a pillowcase, and clear water comes out of a spigot at the bottom to go back into the throwing bucket. Nothing from this system goes down a drain.

Earlier versions of this idea: a two-tier bucket filter with a sink drain on top and a rain barrel spigot in the lower bucket (March), then a three-tote settling cascade that swapped plaster for a hanging pillowcase and then for terracotta pots (July). This page combines them into one stack.

## The Stack, Top to Bottom

Stack height is about 3.5 feet with the pot. Put it somewhere it cannot get bumped, since a full top bucket is heavy.

### 1. Terracotta pot (top, optional dewatering stage)

- An unglazed pot sits down into the mouth of the top bucket with its flared wall resting on the rim.
- Plug the drainage hole with a wad of your own clay.
- The pot wall pulls water out of thick slurry, and the weep water drips into the bucket below instead of onto the floor.
- Most of the outside wall stays in open air above the rim, which keeps the evaporation side working.
- Lift it off when you need to pour new dirty water in.

### 2. Bucket B (filter bucket)

- Drill about 20 to 30 quarter-inch holes in the bottom, kept inside a 5 to 6 inch circle in the center.
- Line it with a pillowcase folded over the rim. For extra support, put a paint strainer bag in first and the pillowcase inside it. The strainer mesh alone is too coarse to stop clay fines.
- Dirty water goes in here. Clay builds up in the pillowcase as a filter cake, which is your reclaim.
- When the cake gets thick, either move it into the terracotta pot or lift the pillowcase out and hang it.

### 3. Bucket A lid (the platform)

- Cut a 6 inch hole in the center of A's lid and snap it on.
- Bucket B sits flat on this lid with its drilled holes centered over the opening.
- This stacks the buckets instead of nesting them, so A keeps its full volume.

### 4. Bucket A (clean water reservoir)

- Install the spigot about 2 inches up from the bottom.
- Fines that sneak through settle below that line, and you draw clearer water off the top.
- Scoop the settled layer into the pillowcase every few weeks.

### Base

Set the whole thing on a cinder block or milk crate so a jug fits under the spigot.

## Tools

- Drill with a `1/4 inch bit` for the drain holes in B.
- `Hole saw` matching the spigot kit's bulkhead size.
- Jigsaw or utility knife for the 6 inch lid opening.

## Build Steps

1. **Drill bucket B.** 20 to 30 quarter-inch holes in the bottom, all inside a 5 to 6 inch circle in the center.
2. **Cut the platform lid.** Cut a 6 inch hole in the center of one lid. Snap it onto bucket A.
3. **Install the spigot.** Hole saw through A's wall about 2 inches up from the bottom, fit the bulkhead, seal with silicone, let it cure.
4. **Line B.** Paint strainer bag first if using one, then the pillowcase folded over the rim.
5. **Stack.** Base, then A with the cut lid, then B centered over the hole, then the terracotta pot in B's mouth. Plug the pot's drainage hole with clay.
6. **Run.** Pour dirty water into B (or thick slurry into the pot). Draw clean water from A's spigot into a jug.

## Use Notes

- The clean water in A goes back into the throwing bucket or sink rinse. Nothing from this system goes down a drain.
- Flow through the pillowcase slows to a drip once the clay cake forms. That is normal and is what makes it filter so fine.
- Keep the second lid on B when not in use so dust stays out.
