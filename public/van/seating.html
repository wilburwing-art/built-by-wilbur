Removable + swiveling seating for the 2016 Transit 130" WB cargo van

Researched 2026-09-20. Prices are US retail, verified against vendor pages the same day unless marked estimate. This topic file is written against the real 2016 Transit cargo van (rear seats being added on purchased floor rails), not exv-130wb-custom; the shell seat data carries over to the custom build and is tabulated at the end.

The constraint that shapes the whole choice

The 2016 Transit is under 10,000 lb GVWR, so FMVSS applies to any seat added to it:

  • Every forward-facing belted passenger position needs a 3-point belt (FMVSS 208 via the anchorage rules).
  • The seat frame, belt anchorages and mounting base must survive FMVSS 207/210 pull testing: 6,000 lb plus 20x the combined seat-and-base weight (JMG Systems).
  • Tested seat + tested anchorage are separate purchases. A certified seat bolted to an untested floor is not a certified installation. Smartfloor and AbiliTrax both require certified or authorized install for their compliance paperwork to be valid.
  • ECE (European) certification is not equivalent. Scopema's TUV mark covers the swivel plate only, and EuroCampers states the Transit unit is pull-tested to FMVSS 207/210, which is the relevant one here.

Practical consequence: a race shell (Tillett B6, Kirkey 41/55, Sparco) carrying a belted road passenger in this van fails the standard. Those seats belong in the custom exv build, where a harness anchors to the cage and the loads leave the seat entirely.

Option tiers

Tier 1: proven removable swivel bases (lightest compliant path)

Koolands captain seat swivel base, 360 deg with quick release - $310 (reg. $350). Second-row base: lever quick release, swivels, mounts on a base plate. Built for Koolands' own 19" reclining captain seat and anything matching its mounting pattern. No FMVSS claim anywhere on the product page, so treat it as the lightweight recreational tier. Weight not published (estimate 25-35 lb based on the class). Pairs with a Koolands 19" reclining captain seat if you want a matched set.

Scopema swivel, 2015-2026 Ford Transit - $349.95 (reg. $399), EuroCampers (CBTO19x). Genuine Scopema, steel, FMVSS 207/210 and CMVSS pull tested, raises the seat only 23 mm. Side-specific, sold per seat; driver side with manual parking brake is $38 more. This is the cab-seat swivel with the best compliance story and the lowest profile. It does not quick-release; it swivels only. Weight of the Transit adapter not published; the older Sprinter Scopema units run 24 lb (2019+) to 32 lb (2007-2018), so 25-30 lb is a reasonable estimate.

Tier 2: certified quick-disconnect seat systems (compliant and removable)

Fenton Mobility Single Step-N-Lock base - $658 (AbiliTrax Step-N-Lock base is $968 at Suburban Seats). NHTSA-compliant quick-disconnect, foot-activated, works with Freedman rigid and flip seats AND factory Ford Transit seats. The catch: it requires a full AbiliTrax floor system (2014-current Transit, seat pods/rails at 12.5" on center), so the base price sits on top of a flooring install.

Step-N-Lock Quick Disconnect Stowable Seat Base - price on request. Requires the separate Freedman Flip Seat, FMVSS tested to 207/210/225. This is the turnkey "pop out the whole belted seat" answer if you buy into AbiliTrax flooring.

Freedman GO-ES (the seat half of tier 2): FMVSS 207/210 and 202A tested, external 3-point belts, Lock-N-Go cushions, flip and foldaway variants, 16"/19" widths. Pricing is quote-based from Freedman; observed street references: GO-ES rigid with Fenton EZ Mount leg $2,170.83 (RVan Conversion), a used 2023 GO-ES double with 3-point belts listed at $1,475 on the Transit forum. MSRP on a single GO-ES flip with 3-pt belt runs roughly $1,200-1,600 estimate.

Tier 3: full certified floor systems (most flexible, heaviest, priciest)

Smartfloor, Ford Transit 130" - $4,241.02 for the base 2-rail system (northwest overland, tax included; 4- and 6-rail packages quoted separately). 123 lb of aluminum floor. ADA and FMVSS compliant, certified-installer required, approved quick-release locking of M1 seats anywhere on the floor, free iPad configurator. This is the buy-once-reconfigure-forever answer and the only one where a seat can move anywhere in the cabin footprint. It needs M1-type seats with the matching locking mechanism.

Adventure Wagon modular interior - Sprinter kit $16,000 at Main Line Overland as a data point for full-system scope; Transit-specific pricing is by quote. Walls, ceiling, floor and L-track as one engineered system. Heavier and much more than a seating project.

Raw floor rails (what the van needs either way)

The 2016 cargo van has no rear seat anchorage; you are buying and installing passenger seat rails or a track system regardless of which seat goes on top. L-track is the conversion standard (Adventure Wagon and others sell L-track by the foot) but plain L-track is not an FMVSS 207/210 anchorage by itself; the belt loads have to resolve through a tested base or a reinforced reinforcement plate under the floor. OEM Transit passenger seat rails from a wrecked passenger wagon are the cheapest anchor for factory Transit seats (forum-standard approach).

Recommendation for the 2016 Transit

Two-seat plan, ~$1,700-2,600:

  • Cab: Scopema Transit swivel under the front passenger seat, $349.95 (+$38 if the driver side goes under a manual brake lever). Compliant, 23 mm raise, no removal.
  • Cabin: one certified removable seat for the belted passenger. Cleanest single-purchase path: Freedman GO-ES single flip with 3-pt belt on a Fenton/AbiliTrax quick-disconnect base, budgeting $1,800-2,600 for the pair, with the AbiliTrax flooring requirement being the real cost driver. Cheaper non-certified path: Koolands swivel-QR base at $310 plus a 19" captain seat, accepting the missing FMVSS paperwork.
  • Skip Smartfloor unless the floorplan will be reconfigured repeatedly; $4,241 plus certified install buys flexibility this van's use case may not need.
