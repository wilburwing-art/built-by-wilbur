🚐 Removable + Swiveling Seating — DIY Dashboard

2016 Ford Transit 130" WB cargo van · researched 2026-09-20 · prices US retail, verified same day unless marked unconfirmed · companion to dreamvan/shared/topics/seating-removable-swivel-2016-transit-130wb.md
The constraint that shapes everything: the 2016 Transit is under 10,000 lb GVWR, so FMVSS applies to any seat you add. Every forward-facing belted passenger needs a 3-point belt, and the seat frame + anchorage must survive the FMVSS 207/210 pull test (6,000 lb plus 20× seat+base weight). Tested seat + tested anchorage are separate purchases — a certified seat bolted to an untested floor is not a certified install. Race shells (Tillett, Kirkey, Sparco) fail this standard for road seats in this van; they belong in the exv harness-to-cage build instead.

The DIY build plan

two seat positions, ~$1,700 to $2,600
Decide the compliance posture first
If anyone ever rides belted in the rear, go the certified route (Freedman GO-ES seat + tested base or rails). The Koolands quick-release base is lighter and a third of the price but carries no FMVSS paperwork — fine for camp + occasional careful use, a real liability decision for daily passengers. This is a family-safety call, not a spec call.
Buy + install rear floor rails or track
The 2016 cargo van has no rear seat anchorage. Options: OEM Transit passenger-wagon rails from a wrecked van (cheapest anchor for factory Transit seats), plain L-track by the foot (not an FMVSS anchorage alone — belt loads must resolve through a tested base or reinforcement under the floor), or full AbiliTrax/Smartfloor flooring. Budget $200 to $800 used OEM, more for track systems.
Cab: Scopema swivel under the front passenger seat
$349.95 from EuroCampers (part CBTO19x), +$38 if the driver side also goes under a manual parking-brake lever. Steel plates, pull-tested to FMVSS 207/210 and CMVSS, raises the seat only 23 mm, rotates 180°. Side-specific, sold per seat. Bolt-in with hand tools; the OEM seat stays, gains rotation. This is the single highest-satisfaction dollar in the whole project.
Rear seat: certified path — Freedman GO-ES + quick-disconnect base
GO-ES single flip with 3-pt belt (FMVSS 207/210/202A tested, Lock-N-Go cushions) on a Fenton Step-N-Lock base ($658) or AbiliTrax base ($968). Catch: Step-N-Lock requires a full AbiliTrax floor system (2014+ Transit, pods/rails 12.5" on center) — the flooring is the real cost driver. Seat alone runs roughly $1,200 to $1,600 new (quote-based); observed $2,170 with Fenton EZ-mount leg. Removability here means foot-activated quick disconnect in a compliant system.
Rear seat: lightweight path — Koolands swivel + QR base
$310 for the 360° swivel base with lever quick release, pairs with a Koolands 19" reclining captain seat (or any seat matching its mounting pattern). Whole pair lands near $600 to $900. Weight not published (estimate 25 to 35 lb for the base). No FMVSS claim — read step 1 again before choosing this for a belted daily passenger.
Skip Smartfloor unless the floorplan will change repeatedly
$4,241.02 for the 130" 2-rail aluminum floor (123 lb, 4/6-rail packages quoted separately), certified installer required, ADA + FMVSS compliant, seats lock anywhere on the floor. It is the buy-once-reconfigure-forever answer and the only system where a seat moves anywhere in the cabin — but for one fixed rear seat plus a cab swivel it is overkill. Adventure Wagon full interior systems ($16k Sprinter data point) are a different project entirely.

Swivel hardware for exv: Scopema-class swivels run 24 to 32 lb, so a custom ring (thin-section slewing bearing, 12 to 16", + pip-pin quick release on the upper race) at a 16 to 20 lb estimate per seat is where the four-seat exv spec (200 lb seats + 20 lb swivels frozen in range.yaml) should be re-baselined. Retention path (pins, pin plate, bearing mount) must be machined or welded metal, never FDM PA-CF. A marine single-post pedestal is the lightest thing on the market and the wrong shape: cantilever load at one bolt circle; the ring-on-floor stack spreads the same load over the full bearing diameter.
