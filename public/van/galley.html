Cabinet lighting: 12 V motion-sensing LED strips

Both cabinets get a run of COB LED tape on the inside of the top front rail, switched by a PIR motion sensor inside the box, fed from the van's factory Customer Connection Point (the Auxiliary Fuse Panel, option 87E on the window sticker in My van.md). No batteries to change and no switch to find in the dark: open a door and the box lights; close it and the strip times out behind the door.

Circuit

CCP battery-fed stud, ring terminal, inline ATC fuse holder with a 7.5 A fuse as close to the stud as its leads allow, 14 AWG duplex to the galley, then a butt-spliced split: one branch to the galley PIR and its strip, one up the wall to the upper PIR and its strip. The reading light, its USB port and the dimmed ambient strip (later sections) ride the same feed, which is why it is 14 AWG and 7.5 A rather than the 16 AWG and 5 A the cabinet strips alone would need. Negative returns to the CCP ground stud or a bolted chassis ground. The cabinet frame is never the return: anodized aluminum conducts poorly across bracketed joints and the frame is not bonded to the chassis.

  • Use a battery-fed stud so the lights work parked; the run-fed studs drop out with the ignition. The BEMM (linked from dreamvan-custom/notes/🚐 My van build.md, line 2513) says which stud is which. The CCP sits under the driver's seat (from memory, confirm on the van).
  • The CCP studs are fused at the panel far above 7.5 A, so the inline fuse is what protects the wire and the sensor's 6 A rating.
  • Voltage drop: 14 AWG stranded copper runs about 2.6 ohm per 1,000 ft. A 20 ft one-way run is 40 ft of loop, 0.10 ohm. The cabinet strips alone draw 0.7 A (0.07 V); with the reading light, a phone on its USB port and the ambient strip all on, the branch peaks near 3.9 A, 0.4 V, about 3% of 12.6 V.
  • Standby: PIR modules of this kind idle at a few mA (est, the listing does not state it). Two at 2 mA is 0.6 Wh a day; 20 door openings at the 1-minute minimum delay is another 2.8 Wh. Call it 3.5 Wh, under 0.3 Ah a day, off the dual AGM starting batteries. Small but real: a month parked costs about 9 Ah.
  • The 1-minute minimum delay runs behind a closed door and costs 0.14 Wh per opening. Set the delay to minimum and any LUX or daylight threshold to "always" if the module has one; a cabinet interior is dark at noon too.

Mounting

  • Strip: 8 mm wide, 3M backing. Wipe the anodized rail with isopropyl and stick the strip to the inside face of the top front rail, on the flat between the slot and the top edge (each flat is about 0.37", 9.4 mm, so the strip fits). It hides behind the door header and throws light down and back. Never on coroplast: polypropylene sheds the tape.
  • Cut only at the printed 25 mm marks; solder or clip the lead at the cut end. The strip is IP20, so it stays at the top of the sink bay above the rim line and away from the drain.
  • PIR: the module's own screw holes onto a #10-32 combo in the rail slot, or VHB. 60 degree cone, 5 to 8 m range; inside a 10" box it sees the whole opening.
  • Wire: the strip leads drop into a corner post's T-slot and run down it to the floor panel, then out through a notch. The slot is a ready-made raceway; a 47" COVER-010-BLK slot cover closes it where the wire shows. In the upper, the lead leaves through the back panel beside a wall bolt.

Interior fabric lining

Every panel face that looks into a cabinet gets the fabric the wall panels wear: Marathon Tweed, Grey Heather, 100% polyolefin, 54" wide, from Perfect Fit (Van wall panels.md bought it there for the wall panels, so check the offcuts from that job before ordering). It stops pots and cans rattling on bare coroplast, deadens the box, and makes the inside match the walls when a door is open.

Adhesive

3M Hi-Strength 90 contact adhesive. Its listing names polyethylene and polypropylene among what it bonds, which is why it is specified over the cheaper cans on the same Home Depot shelf (Super 77 $14.48, Gorilla $12.98, neither checked for polypropylene). Both surfaces get sprayed, since coroplast and the fabric back are both polyolefin, the hardest case for any spray glue: scuff the coroplast with 220 grit, wipe with isopropyl, spray both faces, wait until tacky (about a minute), lay from one edge and roll flat. Do one upper side panel first and pull on it the next day before lining the rest. Coverage per 12.6 oz can is not on the Home Depot page; two cans are specified against 76 sq ft of sprayed surface (both sides of 38). Read the can label and adjust.

Assembly order

  1. Lay out the two side frames flat: post, 16" top rail, 16" bottom rail, post. Square with a framing square before tightening; T-nuts loaded from the rail ends, brackets on the inside faces.
  2. Stand the sides up and join with the four 34" front/back rails. Drop the floor coroplast into the bottom-rail slots before the last rail goes on. Line the floor panel first (step 5) if it is going in lined.
  3. Add the two 32" center posts and the two 16" center depth rails, sliding the divider panel in as the top center rail closes it.
  4. Fit the four 16" drawer-slide rails at 6" and 12" above the floor panel (drawer heights 5" and 9" with 1" of clearance). Mount the slides with the #10-32 combos before the back panels go in.
  5. Line the panels on the bench before any of them goes into a slot: cut the fabric pieces 1/2" inside the panel edges, scuff, wipe, spray both faces, lay and roll, and let them cure overnight. The upper's side panel is the test piece.
  6. Wire while the box is open: stick the 350 mm strip to the inside face of the top front rail over the sink bay, mount the PIR on the divider's top rail aimed at the door, drop both leads down the nearest corner post slot and out through a notch in the floor panel. Slot cover on if the wire shows.
  7. Slide side and back panels in, then run gasket around every panel.
  8. Set the top on, mark from below, drill 1/8" pilots into the bamboo through the six countertop brackets, screw up. Cut the sink hole after the top is fixed so the frame holds it flat.
  9. Build the drawer boxes (15" wide x 16" deep), hang, then fit fronts from the bamboo offcut. Hang the door on the two hinges, set the latch.
  10. Upper: same sequence in miniature. Two 8" x 10" side frames, join with the 28" rails, panels lined and slid in (top, bottom, back, sides), the 700 mm strip on the inside of the top front rail and the PIR at its centre, lead out through the back panel beside a wall-bolt hole. Hang the two doors, set the latches, then through-bolt to the ribs (4 top, 2 bottom) before the galley goes in under it.
  11. In the van: four floor brackets through the subfloor with the 1/4-20 bolts; one rivnut in the nearest wall rib and a bracket from the top back rail to it. Run the 14 AWG from the CCP battery-fed stud through the inline 7.5 A fuse to the galley, splice the branches (both PIR strips, the reading light, the dimmer and its ambient strip), and open both doors to test the sensors before the plumbing goes in.
  12. Dividers before dishes: glue and brad the upper's cubby grid and the drawer inserts on the bench, foam every contact face, and drop them in. The knife block's kerfs are a table-saw job; cut them before the block is glued to anything.
  13. Shower: hole-saw the 2-3/4" wand cup into the bamboo top beside the sink before the top's finish goes on. Pump on the sink-bay floor with its strainer on the tank side, tee after it, one leg to the faucet, one to the wand's 1/2" NPS elbow. Pump run on its own 12 AWG from the CCP through the 10 A fuse and the galley toggle. Rod bracket on the upper's aisle-end post; hang the curtain, open the basin, run water into it and drain it through cap 2 before the first shower.
  14. Outside pockets last, once the lined panels and doors are in: button heads into drop-in T-nuts at each corner, adhesive strips pressed on, pouch buckles over their studs, hook-and-loop behind them. Plumb the sink drain last of all.

Open items

  • Measure the owned sink (outside rim, bowl depth, drain position) before ordering the bamboo, since the sink dictates whether 18" deep holds.
  • Measure the sled (already on the priority list) and confirm 18" of cabinet still leaves the load path.
  • Rivnut tool: check the toolbox before buying one.
  • TNUTZ shipping: put the cart together and read the UPS quote; it was not checked here.
  • Measure the run from the CCP under the driver's seat to the galley; 30 ft of wire covers 20 ft one-way plus the upper branch, and the 50 ft roll is the fallback.
  • Read the BEMM CCP page for which studs are battery-fed and their stud size before buying ring terminals.
  • Check the wall-panel offcuts for Grey Heather before ordering 4 yards, and hold a swatch against the installed panels: Perfect Fit warns colour names drift between runs.
  • Pull-test the first lined panel a day after gluing. If it peels, switch the rest to wrap-and-slot.
  • Measure the fresh tank (still ~ in vehicle.yaml); at 2 to 3 gal a shower it sets how many showers a fill gives.
  • Read the SEAFLO box for its fuse and draw before buying the 10 A fuse; 10 A is from memory.
  • Measure the pool folded before deciding whether it quarters onto the sink bay floor or stands halved against the back panel.
  • Confirm which coupling the pool's cap 2 carries (GHT thread or a Gardena-style click) before buying the drain hose end.
  • Decide where the reading light points (bench or bed) before drilling the upper's rail; the wall panel is the alternate mount.
