Uppers: 30" x 10" x 10" cabinet

TNUTZ prices are verified 2026-09-20 except the extrusion line, extrapolated from the EX-1010 variation table (six verified lengths, 16" to 96") until you put the exact cuts in a cart.

Uppers open items: (1) put the 8", 10", and 28" lengths in a TNUTZ cart to read real per-length prices; (2) confirm the wall rib positions before cutting any rail to length, the frame can be re-spaced but the extrusion cannot grow; (3) decide whether the coroplast back needs a thin rigid backing where the wall bolts pass; (4) confirm the two 15" door clearances against the van wall and ceiling before ordering panels.

Lights + lining: both cabinets

12 V COB LED strip on the inside of each top front rail, switched by a PIR motion sensor inside the box, fed from the van's factory Customer Connection Point (Auxiliary Fuse Panel, option 87E) through an inline 5 A fuse. 700 mm in the upper, 350 mm over the sink bay, 8.4 W and 0.7 A total, about 3.5 Wh a day with the sensors idling. Every interior panel face lined with the same Marathon Tweed the wall panels wear (Grey Heather, 54" wide, 100% polyolefin), glued with 3M 90, the one spray can on the shelf whose listing names polypropylene. Prices read 2026-09-20 off Amazon, TNUTZ, Perfect Fit and Home Depot.

Lights + lining open items: (1) measure the CCP-to-galley wire run, 30 ft covers 20 ft one-way plus the upper branch, the 50 ft roll ($25.49) is the fallback; (2) read the BEMM CCP page for which studs are battery-fed and the stud size before buying ring terminals, the lights must be on a battery-fed stud to work parked; (3) check the wall-panel offcuts for Grey Heather before ordering 4 yards, and hold a swatch against the installed panels, Perfect Fit warns colour names drift between runs; (4) glue one upper side panel first and pull on it the next day, polyolefin fabric on polypropylene sheet is the hardest case for spray glue, and the fallback is wrap-and-slot with no glue and no gasket on those panels; (5) 3M 90 coverage per 12.6 oz can is not on the Home Depot page, two cans are specified against 76 sq ft of sprayed surface, read the label and adjust. Full spec, cut sizes, circuit and mounting detail in galley-cabinet.md.

Pull-out shower: wand, folding rod, curtain, folding basin

Cold water, no mixer, everything stows in the sink bay. A Scandvik recessed wand deck-mounted in the bamboo top beside the sink (2-3/4" hole, 6 ft hose, trigger is the valve). The 69" 5-fold rod from the request mounts on the upper's aisle-end post through its T-slot and swings out 90° over the aisle with a clear PEVA curtain. The basin is the Jasonwell folding dog pool, S, 32" across with 8" walls, drained through its cap 2 hose connector out the slider door or into the grey line. A SEAFLO 33-series pump on its own 12 AWG, 10 A circuit feeds the wand and the sink faucet, which retires the Whale foot pump. Hot water is a separate project.

Shower open items: (1) folded size of the pool is on no listing, a 32" disc quartered is about 16" x 16" x 5" est, exactly the sink bay floor, so measure on arrival and expect to stand it halved (16" x 32") against the back panel; (2) read the SEAFLO box for its fuse and draw, 10 A is from memory; (3) confirm whether cap 2 is a GHT thread or a Gardena-style click before buying the hose end; (4) the fresh tank is still "~" in vehicle.yaml, and a navy shower is 2 to 3 gal est; (5) the upper's post top sits near 62" off the floor, fine for a hand-held wand, a ceiling-rib bracket gets 74" if wanted; (6) the 57" 4-fold rod ($39.59, B0DJBT728L) is enough for a 32" basin if the diamond stays tight.

Outside storage, reading + ambient light, EarthRoamer-style dividers

Elastic mesh pockets on the galley's aisle end and sink-bay door, mesh zipper pouches hung from button-head studs in the post slots (aisle end bottom, both upper ends), every corner into a drop-in T-nut because nothing adhesive holds a loaded pocket on polypropylene. A flexible-neck reading light through the upper's bottom rail and a 3000K warm COB strip in two 28" runs (counter wash under the upper, ceiling bounce above it) on a 4 A PWM dimmer, both on the lights branch. Slotted 1/4" ply dividers with foam bumpers: mugs two deep and bowl stacks in the upper, plates on edge and pots in the 9" drawer, a kerfed knife block in the 5" drawer. Plates do not stand in an 8"-tall upper, so they live in the drawer.

Storage + light open items: (1) decide where the reading light points (bench or bed) before drilling the upper's rail, the wall panel is the alternate mount; (2) the FCOB listing gives 480 LEDs/m and no wattage, 10 W/m is an estimate, the 4 A dimmer has headroom either way; (3) growing the upper to 12" tall (about $2 more extrusion est) would let dinner plates stand in it, declined for now; (4) the knife block's 3/8" kerfs at 1" pitch are a table-saw cut, do them before gluing.
