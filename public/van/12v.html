The house-battery-and-isolator half of the old note never got built, and does not need to: the Yeti is the house battery and the Victron is the isolator. What remains unbuilt from that note is the 6-circuit block, the 100 A breaker and the VSR. Treat those paragraphs of the note as history.

The ceiling is the Yeti's port, not the block

Every 12 V output port on the Yeti 1000 Lithium is rated the same:

Port Rating
6 mm output 12 V, up to 10 A (120 W max)
12 V car port 12 V, up to 10 A (120 W max)
12 V Power Pole output 12 V, up to 10 A (120 W max)
AC inverter 120 VAC, 12.5 A (1500 W, 3000 W surge)

So the Blue Sea block's 100 A rating describes the block and says nothing about what it can deliver here. Whatever port feeds it hands it 10 A. That one number decides everything below.

Unresolved and worth settling with a meter: whether 10 A is per port or shared across the whole 12 V section. Goal Zero publishes it per port; if it is actually a shared 120 W budget, then feeding a second branch from a second port is not the headroom it looks like. Test by running a known load on two ports at once and watching for a shutdown.

The port is already oversubscribed

Add the middle of each range and the four existing loads come to 13.3 A on a 10 A port:

  fan on high         3.0 A  ████████████
  Starlink average    3.3 A  █████████████
  8 pucks             2.0 A  ████████
                     ------
  all four           13.3 A  |-------- 10 A port --------|  over by 3.3

Drop the fan and it is still 10.3 A. Only the fridge's duty cycle keeps this from being constant: the compressor is off most of the time in mild weather, so the four coincide only when it restarts. That is also exactly when a shutdown is least welcome.

So this is not a question about whether the galley may add load. The existing four already exceed the port whenever the compressor runs, and what to do about that is the decision below.

Two details behind the numbers. Starlink's sheet gives an average rather than a peak, and separately an input rating of 60 W, so 5.0 A is the number to size to and 3.3 A is what it spends. And the fridge is duty cycled while the fan and Starlink are not, so on a warm night those two are the larger load in watt-hours: 3 A and 3.3 A continuously against a compressor at roughly a tenth duty.

Splitting it

The Yeti publishes 10 A on each of three 12 V outputs, so the obvious fix is to stop hanging everything off one. Whether that is real headroom is the open question at the top of this doc, and it has stopped being academic: if the 10 A is a shared 120 W budget for the whole 12 V section, then no arrangement of ports helps and the answer is the inverter or fewer loads. Settle it with a meter before rewiring anything.

If it is per port, the split that matches the loads is Starlink alone on its own port (5.0 A sized, 3.3 A spent) and the fridge, fan and pucks on the block (10.0 A worst case, still tight, so watch the fan on high).

Starlink Mini takes 12 to 48 V directly, so it does not need the inverter, and running it on AC through the Yeti's inverter would cost the conversion loss plus the inverter's idle draw for no benefit.

Where the galley circuits land

galley-cabinet.md put both new circuits on the CCP battery-fed studs with inline fuses. That stays, and the pump's case is now settled rather than provisional:

  • The water pump must not go on the Yeti block. The SEAFLO SFDP1-030-045-33 draws 6.74 A at its duty point and 8.0 A maximum (SEAFLO's own 33-series table). On a 10 A port that is the entire budget, and it would cut out the fridge every time someone rinsed a plate. It stays on the CCP, on its own 12 AWG and 10 A fuse, where the engine battery and the alternator carry it.
  • The cabinet lights could move, and it is not worth doing. The two PIR strips draw 0.7 A, and the reading light plus ambient strip add about 3.1 A est at once. Against a port that the fridge and fan already share, that is a real bite for a load that costs about 3.5 Wh a day. Leave them on the CCP with the 14 AWG and 7.5 A fuse already specified.
  • What the CCP branch does cost is standby off the starting batteries, about 9 Ah over a parked month (galley doc). If the van sits for weeks, pull the 7.5 A fuse.

Two things to verify on the van, both of which change the charge rate a lot:

  1. Which input port each source is plugged into. Two 100 W panels on an 8 mm port are capped at 120 W. If the Victron and the panels share the Power Pole input through a splitter, they share its budget too.
  2. The Victron's configured output voltage. The Yeti only accepts 14 to 22 V. A 12/12-30 in charger mode drops to a float around 13.5 V once it thinks the battery is full, which falls below the Yeti's window and stops the charge with nothing reported. Set it to power-supply mode at a fixed voltage above 14 V. The note's own saved write-up ran exactly this way, with input lockout at 13.2 / 13.4 V so it disengages when the engine stops.

Rough energy balance

1045 Wh peak, call it about 950 Wh usable.

An earlier version of this doc put the fridge at roughly 600 Wh a day by multiplying its running draw by an assumed duty cycle. Two reported figures say it is closer to 160 Wh: 0.16 kWh per 24 hours quoted by a reseller for a Costway of this family, and 13 Ah per 24 hours measured by an owner in a truck camper. Both imply about a tenth duty in mild conditions. Heat, a warm load, and a lid opened often all raise it, so treat 160 Wh as the floor and somewhere near 400 Wh as a hot-day figure. Neither is a manufacturer number.

Against that, the continuous loads are the surprise. Three amps of fan is 36 W, so a night of it is about 290 Wh, more than the fridge spends in a mild day. Starlink at 25 to 40 W is the same order: a four-hour evening is 100 to 160 Wh, and a full day of it would be 600 to 960 Wh, which is the whole pack.

200 W of solar at 4.5 peak-sun hours and 80 percent efficiency is about 720 Wh on a good summer day and a small fraction of that in a Wasatch December. An hour of driving through the Victron adds 240 to 360 Wh depending on which port limit is real.

So the pack is comfortable for the fridge and tight for the radio. Fridge plus a night of fan is roughly 450 Wh against 950 usable and either charging path covers it; add Starlink for a working day and the pack is the constraint rather than the port. For everything else the constraint is the instantaneous 10 A port, which is why the galley's 3.5 Wh of lighting is noise and the pump's 8 A is disqualifying.

Open items

  • Identify the puck lights and their per-fixture wattage, or clamp the dimmer's feed. 8 x 3 W and 8 x 1.5 W are a whole amp apart on a port with none to spare.
  • Confirm which Yeti input port the Victron and the solar are on, and whether that port is the 240 W or 360 W one.
  • Confirm the Victron model and its configured output voltage and mode.
