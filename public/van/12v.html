The house-battery-and-isolator half of the old note never got built, and does not need to: the Yeti is the house battery and the Victron is the isolator. What remains unbuilt from that note is the 6-circuit block, the 100 A breaker and the VSR. Treat those paragraphs of the note as history.

The ceiling is the Yeti's port, not the block

Every 12 V output port on the Yeti 1000 Lithium is rated the same:

Port Rating
6 mm output 12 V, up to 10 A (120 W max)
12 V car port 12 V, up to 10 A (120 W max)
12 V Power Pole output 12 V, up to 10 A (120 W max)
AC inverter 120 VAC, 12.5 A (1500 W, 3000 W surge)

So the Blue Sea block's 100 A rating describes the block and says nothing about what it can deliver here. Whatever port feeds it hands it 10 A. That one number decides everything below.

Unresolved and worth settling with a meter: whether 10 A is per port or shared across the whole 12 V section. Goal Zero publishes it per port; if it is actually a shared 120 W budget, then feeding a second branch from a second port is not the headroom it looks like. Test by running a known load on two ports at once and watching for a shutdown.

Where the galley circuits land

galley-cabinet.md put both new circuits on the CCP battery-fed studs with inline fuses. That stays, and the pump's case is now settled rather than provisional:

  • The water pump must not go on the Yeti block. The SEAFLO SFDP1-030-045-33 draws 6.74 A at its duty point and 8.0 A maximum (SEAFLO's own 33-series table). On a 10 A port that is the entire budget, and it would cut out the fridge every time someone rinsed a plate. It stays on the CCP, on its own 12 AWG and 10 A fuse, where the engine battery and the alternator carry it.
  • The cabinet lights could move, and it is not worth doing. The two PIR strips draw 0.7 A, and the reading light plus ambient strip add about 3.1 A est at once. Against a port that the fridge and fan already share, that is a real bite for a load that costs about 3.5 Wh a day. Leave them on the CCP with the 14 AWG and 7.5 A fuse already specified.
  • What the CCP branch does cost is standby off the starting batteries, about 9 Ah over a parked month (galley doc). If the van sits for weeks, pull the 7.5 A fuse.

Charging

Path Input port Published limit
Alternator, via the Victron Power Pole charging input 14 to 22 V, up to 30 A (360 W max) per Goal Zero's spec table; B&H lists the same port at 20 A / 240 W, so read the label on the unit before sizing anything to it
Alternator, if it landed on the 8 mm port instead 8 mm charging input 14 to 22 V, up to 10 A (120 W max)
Solar, 200 W whichever port it is on the 8 mm port would throw away roughly 80 W of the 200; the Power Pole input would not

Two things to verify on the van, both of which change the charge rate a lot:

  1. Which input port each source is plugged into. Two 100 W panels on an 8 mm port are capped at 120 W. If the Victron and the panels share the Power Pole input through a splitter, they share its budget too.
  2. The Victron's configured output voltage. The Yeti only accepts 14 to 22 V. A 12/12-30 in charger mode drops to a float around 13.5 V once it thinks the battery is full, which falls below the Yeti's window and stops the charge with nothing reported. Set it to power-supply mode at a fixed voltage above 14 V. The note's own saved write-up ran exactly this way, with input lockout at 13.2 / 13.4 V so it disengages when the engine stops.

Rough energy balance

1045 Wh peak, call it about 950 Wh usable. A compressor fridge at 30 to 40 percent duty is roughly 600 Wh a day (est, from the 4 to 6 A above), so the fridge alone is most of the pack. 200 W of solar at 4.5 peak-sun hours and 80 percent efficiency is about 720 Wh on a good summer day and a small fraction of that in a Wasatch December. An hour of driving through the Victron adds 240 to 360 Wh depending on which port limit is real.

That balance is why the galley's 3.5 Wh of lighting is noise and the pump's 8 A is not: the constraint here is the instantaneous 10 A port, not the daily watt-hours.
