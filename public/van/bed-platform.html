Aluminum honeycomb bed platform: build guide

Written 2026-09-30. Working copy with the end-view drawing: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/ee036884-b578-4366-9dab-3cd3ee89aafe

Start here

Both beds use the same parts: aluminum honeycomb panels resting on 1" T-slot rails bolted to the van's wall ribs. Build Config A first; Config B reuses its wall rails.

Measure the van first

Published numbers disagree by 1 to 2 inches, so measure your own van before ordering anything.

Checklist (tape measure, pencil, phone camera):

  • Wall to wall at 14", 24", 36" and 42" above the floor, at the rear doors and 72" forward. Write all eight numbers down.
  • The same eight numbers again, measured to the face of the finished wall panels (or where they will be).
  • Floor to ceiling at the center and 12" from each wall.
  • Top of each wheel well above the floor.
  • Where the vertical wall ribs are: distance of each rib's center from the rear door frame. Photograph each one.
  • Rib depth (how far the rib sticks out from the outer skin).
  • Anything in the way at bed height: window, seat belt anchor, wiring, the sliding door track.
  • Everyone's height who will sleep in it.

The narrowest of the eight wall-to-wall numbers sets the bed length in Config A.

Config A: east-west queen-ish

A 60" x 66" platform across the back of the van, in two 30" x 66" panels, sitting on three rails. Total panel weight is about 38 lb; the same platform in 18 mm Baltic birch would be about 69 lb.

  • Panel length is set by the narrowest wall-to-wall number, minus 1/2" of clearance. 66" is a placeholder.
  • Why the center rail: Flatiron's data only covers a 4 x 8 panel held on all four edges. A panel held on two edges over 66" is a case no source here covers, so the design halves the span instead of trusting it.
  • Sit-up room: at a 30" bed height with a 4" mattress and a 70" ceiling, about 35" above the mattress.

Cut plan: a stock 48" x 96" sheet holds one 30" x 66" piece, so Config A needs 2 sheets, leaving two 18" x 66" offcuts for shelves. A custom 60" x 96" sheet (Flatiron makes up to 5' x 12' on request) would hold both pieces; ask for that price.

Config B: four portaledge-style twins

Four bunks running front to back, two on each wall, stacked. Each bunk hangs from a rail on the wall. The upper bunks are held level by two straps from the ceiling, like a climber's portaledge; the lower bunks stand on two fold-down legs, because their straps would have to pass through the bunk above. Folded, each bunk swings up flat against the wall and the floor is clear.

  • Weight: four 24" x 72" panels are 48 sq ft, about 66 lb; four 28" panels about 77 lb (Flatiron's 44 lb per 4 x 8 sheet).

Tools

The easiest path is to have Flatiron CNC-cut the panels (router ±0.008"; saw ±0.063"), so honeycomb is never cut at home.

  • Tape measure, pencil, square, 4' level
  • Drill/driver and bit for the rivnut hole size
  • 1/4-20 rivnut tool
  • Hex keys (5/32" fits 1/4-20 button heads) and a 7/16" wrench or socket
  • Miter saw or hacksaw with a non-ferrous blade, only if cutting T-slot bars (TNUTZ cuts to length)
  • Circular or track saw with a non-ferrous blade, only if cutting honeycomb
  • File or deburring tool
  • Clamps, two
  • A helper for lifting panels (one 30" x 66" panel is about 19 lb, awkward rather than heavy)

Build steps: Config A

One action per step. The next unticked box is where you are.

Phase 1: Decide and order (1 to 2 hours, then wait for shipping)

  • Finish the measuring checklist.
  • Pick the bed height (30" to 36").
  • Write the panel length: narrowest wall-to-wall at that height, minus 1/2".
  • Email Flatiron (sales@flatironpanelproducts.com, 303-664-9601) for two 30" x panel-length pieces, CNC cut, edges closed, inserts at the hold-down points. Ask the price of a custom 60" x 96" sheet too.
  • Order the T-slot bars cut to length from TNUTZ: two wall rails and one center rail at 60", two legs at bed height minus 1-3/4".
  • Order the brackets, T-nut screws and rivnuts.

Phase 2: Wall rails (about 3 hours)

  • Mark the top of the rail on both walls at three ribs: bed height minus 3/4" (the panel).
  • Check the marks are level across the van with the level on a straight board.
  • Drill the first rivnut hole in the first rib.
  • Set the first rivnut and test it with a bolt.
  • Set the rest: at least two per rib the rail crosses.
  • Slide T-nuts into a wall rail, one per bracket.
  • Bolt the brackets to the rivnuts, then the rail to the brackets.
  • Level the rail and tighten.
  • Repeat the last three steps on the other wall.

Phase 3: Center rail and legs (about 2 hours)

  • Join each leg to the center rail with two brackets.
  • Stand the frame in place, centered between the walls.
  • Check the center rail is level with the wall rails.
  • Bolt the leg feet to the floor (measure what is under the floor first).

Phase 4: Panels (about 2 hours)

  • Dry-fit the rear panel on the three rails.
  • Mark the hold-down points where the panel meets each rail.
  • Bolt the rear panel down through its inserts.
  • Dry-fit the front panel.
  • Decide: bolted down, or hinged to the rear panel to lift for garage access.
  • Fit the hinge or the bolts.
  • Lie on it. Bounce on it. Check nothing creaks or moves.

Phase 5: Finish (1 hour)

  • End caps on any exposed rail end.
  • Mattress on.
  • Drive a bumpy road; re-check every bolt.

Build steps: Config B

Build one bunk completely, sleep on it, then build the other three the same way.

Phase 1: Decide and order (1 to 2 hours, then wait for shipping)

  • Finish the measuring checklist.
  • Pick the bunk width: 24" (cheaper, 18" aisle) or 28" (roomier, 10" aisle).
  • Pick the two heights (proposed: 16" lower, 44" upper, to the panel top).
  • Email Flatiron for four bunk panels, CNC cut, edges closed, with inserts for the hinge and the strap or leg points.
  • Order four 72" T-slot rails from TNUTZ, plus brackets, T-nut screws and rivnuts.
  • Order the ceiling L-track, the straps, the fold-down legs and the hinges.

Phase 2: First bunk only, a lower one (about 4 hours)

  • Mark the top of the lower rail on one wall: lower bunk height minus 3/4".
  • Set rivnuts in each rib the rail crosses, two per rib.
  • Bolt the rail to the wall with brackets and level it.
  • Fit the hinge along the rail's top edge.
  • Bolt the bunk panel's wall edge to the hinge.
  • Fit the two fold-down legs under the panel's outer front and back corners.
  • Fold the legs down to the floor.
  • Check the panel is level and adjust the legs.
  • Swing it up to the stowed position and strap it to the wall.

Phase 3: Test (one night)

  • Sleep on it.
  • Note anything that creaked, sagged or rattled while driving.
  • Fix it on this bunk before copying the design.

Phase 4: The other three (about 6 hours)

  • Upper bunk, same wall: mark at upper height minus 3/4", fit the rail and hinge as in Phase 2, then fit one run of L-track to the ceiling above its outer edge and hang it on two straps instead of legs.
  • Lower bunk, other wall.
  • Upper bunk, other wall (the second L-track run goes in here).

Phase 5: Finish (1 hour)

  • End caps, pads on.
  • Stow all four, drive a bumpy road, re-check every bolt and strap.

Safety

No source here rates this exact setup (panel on rails, rails on rivnuts in the ribs), so the design adds margin and testing proves it.

  • Load: Flatiron's only load figure is 0.014" of sag under 500 lb on a 4 x 8 sheet held on all four edges. Config A halves the span with a center rail; Config B bunks span 24" to 28". Before anyone sleeps on it, load each panel with about 1.5x the heaviest sleeper (sandbags or water jugs) for an hour and check for sag or creaks.
  • Point loads: the core resists crushing up to 150 psi. A knee or a heel is far below that; a chair leg or a jack is not.
  • Crash: everything up high has to stay put in a crash. Bolt panels down; never lay them loose on the rails. Strap stowed bunks to the wall. Check Ford's Transit body builder guidance for approved wall attachment points before drilling ribs.
  • Straps: rated working load at least 300 lb each, covering a 250 lb sleeper plus the panel with margin across 2 straps (a design choice, not a published rule).
  • Heat: the panel glue is rated -50°F to 250°F; a parked car in the sun measured 116°F inside and 157°F on the dashboard (ASU study).
  • Fire: the stock panels are A+ under ASTM E84 (a building test). The mattress foam is the real fire load; FMVSS 302 (no faster than 102 mm per minute) is the benchmark for vehicle interiors (49 CFR 571.302).
  • Pinch points: hinged panels and fold-down legs pinch fingers. Lift from the edge, never from the hinge side.
