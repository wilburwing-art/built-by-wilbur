Aluminum honeycomb bed platform: build guide

Written 2026-09-30. Working copy with the end-view drawing: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/ee036884-b578-4366-9dab-3cd3ee89aafe

Start here

Both beds use the same parts: aluminum honeycomb panels on a 1" T-slot frame that stands on the van floor. Revised 2026-10-02: the first draft hung the beds from the wall ribs and ceiling. Ford's body builder manual rules that out (see "What Ford allows" below), so both layouts are now floor-standing frames with the walls used only to steady them.

Checklist (tape measure, pencil, phone camera):

  • Wall to wall at 14", 24", 36" and 42" above the floor, at the rear doors and 72" forward. Write all eight numbers down.
  • The same eight numbers again, measured to the face of the finished wall panels (or where they will be).
  • Floor to ceiling at the center and 12" from each wall.
  • Top of each wheel well above the floor.
  • Where the vertical wall ribs are: distance of each rib's center from the rear door frame. Photograph each one.
  • Rib depth (how far the rib sticks out from the outer skin).
  • Anything in the way at bed height: window, seat belt anchor, wiring, the sliding door track.
  • Everyone's height who will sleep in it.

The narrowest of the eight wall-to-wall numbers sets the bed length in Config A.

What Ford allows

From Ford's Transit Body and Equipment Mounting Manual, section 5.3.1 Racking Systems (2020 North America edition; the 2016 edition was not read):

  • "Upper fixing locations are not structural and take a 30kg maximum load only." That is 66 lb per wall point, far less than a sleeper.
  • "Frames should be rigid, self-supporting and bolted through the floor, use reinforcements on the underfloor."
  • "Load compartment tie downs can also be used for additional fixing locations." The factory D-ring points are the no-drill anchors.
  • "Do not drill into closed frame body members." Re-paint and seal every drilled edge.
  • Check the manual's "precautionary drill zones" figures before drilling the cargo walls: wiring runs behind them.
  • "No load bearing fixing to the lining only", and through bolts get a spreader plate.

So: the frame carries the sleepers to the floor. Wall ties only stop the frame from swaying, and nothing hangs from the ceiling.

Config B: four fold-up twins

Four bunks running front to back, two on each wall, stacked. A climber's portaledge hangs from above; Ford's 66 lb limit on wall and roof points rules that out for a person's weight, so these keep the fold-flat idea and stand on the floor. Each side is a frame of three posts against the wall carrying two rails. Each bunk hinges on its rail and stands on two fold-down legs at the aisle edge. Folded, the bunk swings up flat against the wall and its legs fold against it.

Tools

The easiest path is to have Flatiron CNC-cut the panels (router ±0.008"; saw ±0.063"), so honeycomb is never cut at home.

  • Tape measure, pencil, square, 4' level
  • Drill/driver and bit for the rivnut hole size
  • 1/4-20 rivnut tool
  • Hex keys (5/32" fits 1/4-20 button heads) and a 7/16" wrench or socket
  • Miter saw or hacksaw with a non-ferrous blade, only if cutting T-slot bars (TNUTZ cuts to length)
  • Circular or track saw with a non-ferrous blade, only if cutting honeycomb
  • File or deburring tool
  • Clamps, two
  • A helper for lifting panels (one 30" x 66" panel is about 19 lb, awkward rather than heavy)

Build steps: Config A

One action per step. The next unticked box is where you are.

Phase 1: Decide and order (1 to 2 hours, then wait for shipping)

  • Finish the measuring checklist.
  • Pick the bed height (30" to 36").
  • Write the panel length: narrowest wall-to-wall at that height, minus 1/2".
  • Find the factory tie-down points on the floor and note which ones a leg could bolt to.
  • Email Flatiron (sales@flatironpanelproducts.com, 303-664-9601) for two 30" x panel-length pieces, CNC cut, edges closed, inserts at the hold-down points. Ask the price of a custom 60" x 96" sheet too. (A first quote request went out 2026-10-02.)
  • Order the T-slot bars cut to length from TNUTZ: three rails at 60", six legs at bed height minus 1-3/4", two cross ties at the rail spacing.
  • Order the brackets, T-nut screws, rivnuts and the mattress.

Phase 2: Build the frame on the garage floor (about 3 hours)

  • Bolt two legs to each rail with two brackets per leg.
  • Stand the three rail-and-leg pieces up, side rails about 46" apart, center rail between them.
  • Join them with the two cross ties, front and back.
  • Check the frame is square: measure both diagonals; they should match.
  • Put a drop of thread locker on each bolt and tighten.

Phase 3: Fix the frame in the van (about 3 hours)

  • Carry the frame in and set it between the wheel wells' inboard faces.
  • Check the rails are level front to back and side to side; shim a foot if not.
  • Bolt each foot down: to a factory tie-down point where one lines up, otherwise through the floor with a spreader plate underneath (look under the van at each spot first).
  • Seal and paint every drilled hole.
  • Mark four wall-tie spots on ribs near the frame corners, clear of the wiring zones in Ford's figures.
  • Set a rivnut at each and bolt a bracket from the rib to the frame.

Phase 4: Panels (about 2 hours)

  • Dry-fit the rear panel on the three rails.
  • Mark the hold-down points where the panel meets each rail.
  • Bolt the rear panel down through its inserts.
  • Dry-fit the front panel.
  • Decide: bolted down, or hinged to the rear panel to lift for garage access.
  • Fit the hinge or the bolts.

Phase 5: Test and finish (1 to 2 hours)

  • Load each panel with about 1.5x the heaviest sleeper for an hour (water jugs: 8.34 lb per gallon).
  • Push the loaded frame sideways. If it sways, add diagonal braces before going further.
  • End caps on any exposed rail end.
  • Mattress on.
  • Drive a bumpy road; re-check every bolt.

Build steps: Config B

Build one lower bunk completely, sleep on it, then build the rest the same way.

Phase 1: Decide and order (1 to 2 hours, then wait for shipping)

  • Finish the measuring checklist.
  • Pick the bunk width: 24" (cheaper, 18" aisle) or 28" (roomier, 10" aisle).
  • Pick the two heights (proposed: 16" lower, 44" upper, to the panel top).
  • Email Flatiron for four bunk panels, CNC cut, edges closed, with inserts for the hinge and the leg pivots.
  • Order from TNUTZ: six posts, four 72" rails, four lower legs, four upper legs, plus brackets, T-nut screws and rivnuts.
  • Order the hinges, leg pivots, stow straps and four mattresses.

Phase 2: One side's wall frame (about 3 hours)

  • Bolt the lower and upper rails to three posts on the garage floor, with thread locker.
  • Stand the frame against the wall, posts fore and aft of the wheel well.
  • Bolt each post's foot to the floor or a factory tie-down point; seal any drilled hole.
  • Tie each post to a rib with one rivnut and bracket.

Phase 3: First bunk, a lower one (about 3 hours)

  • Fit the hinge along the lower rail.
  • Bolt the bunk panel's wall edge to the hinge.
  • Fit the two fold-down legs under the panel's aisle-edge corners.
  • Fold the legs down to the floor and check the panel is level.
  • Load-test it: 1.5x the heaviest sleeper for an hour.
  • Swing it up to the stowed position and strap it to the posts.

Phase 4: Test (one night)

  • Sleep on it.
  • Note anything that creaked, sagged or rattled while driving.
  • Fix it on this bunk before copying the design.

Phase 5: The other three (about 8 hours)

  • Upper bunk, same side: hinge on the upper rail, long legs to the floor just outside the lower bunk's edge. Load-test it.
  • Other side: wall frame (Phase 2), then lower and upper bunks.

Phase 6: Finish (1 hour)

  • End caps, pads on.
  • Stow all four, drive a bumpy road, re-check every bolt and strap.

Safety

No source here rates this exact frame, so the design adds margin and a load test proves it.

  • Walls and roof: Ford limits upper wall fixing points to 30 kg (66 lb) each and calls them not structural. Nothing that holds a person hangs from them.
  • Load: Flatiron's only load figure is 0.014" of sag under 500 lb on a 4 x 8 sheet held on all four edges. Config A's rails are about 23" apart; Config B bunks span 24" to 28". Load each panel with about 1.5x the heaviest sleeper for an hour and check for sag, sway or creaks.
  • Point loads: the core resists crushing up to 150 psi. A knee or a heel is far below that; a chair leg or a jack is not.
  • Crash: everything has to stay put. Bolt panels to the frame and the frame to the floor; never lay panels loose. Strap stowed bunks and mattresses.
  • Drilling: check under the floor (fuel tank, lines, wiring) and Ford's wiring-zone figures for the walls before any hole. Seal and paint each one.
  • Heat: the panel glue is rated -50°F to 250°F; a parked car in the sun measured 116°F inside and 157°F on the dashboard (ASU study).
  • Fire: the stock panels are A+ under ASTM E84 (a building test). The mattress is the real fire load; FMVSS 302 (no faster than 102 mm per minute) is the benchmark for vehicle interiors (49 CFR 571.302).
  • Pinch points: hinged panels and fold-down legs pinch fingers. Lift from the edge, never from the hinge side.
